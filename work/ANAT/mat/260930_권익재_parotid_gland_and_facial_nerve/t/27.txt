6. Looking for trunk of facial n.
•
The assistant watches the patient’s face from this time 
onward and reports any sign of movement or 
“twitching” of the facial musculature.
•
Place a finger on the mastoid tip and use the position 
of the cartilaginous pointer and the superior posterior 
edge of the digastric muscle to identify the location of 
the facial nerve.
•
By using amosquito-curved clamp to elevate tissues 
layer by layer from a posterior to an anterior direction 
and slowly separating the fascia and gland, the 
surgeon can easily see the nerve as it enters the 
parotid gland. This white structure, roughly 2 to 3 mm 
in width, will not be confused with other structures.
•
The branching point (pes anserinus) of the temporal 
facial and cervical facial branches confirms that the 
structure is the facial nerve.

Mrs. C is your 65-year-old dental patient. It has been one year since her last dental 
appointment. Nine months ago, her physician prescribed Solifenacin (VESIcare) 
for her overactive bladder. After starting at a dose of 5mg/day, the dose was 
increased to 10mg/day, and the medication has been effective in reducing her 
urinary urgency. She reports that she can tolerate solifenacin reasonably well; 
however, she occasionally has dry eyes and especially notices xerostomia that is 
most evident at night. She sometimes has to keep a glass of water on her 
nightstand at night to relieve the dry mouth. Mrs. C has had good oral health in 
the past and has not had active caries in the last several years. At this appointment, 
her oral soft tissue looks dry but, otherwise, normal. Incipient root surface carious 
lesions are observed on the facial surfaces of tooth numbers 4, 5, and 11. You 
deduce that the xerostomia may be contributing to the increase in caries. 
Would you include a drug sialogogue (a drug that promotes the secretion of 
saliva, 타액분비촉진제) in managing Mrs. C’s oral condition?
*Solifenacin: A competitive cholinergic receptor antagonist, selective for 
the M3 receptor subtype (less dry mouth than the nonselective agents).
-
Pharmacology and Therapeutics for Dentistry, 7th edition. Mosby, 2016

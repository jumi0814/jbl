Muscarinic Cholinergic Toxicity
• Acute toxicity with direct muscarinic agents is often due to 
ingestion of toxic mushrooms (e.g., mushrooms in the 
genus Inocybe) and drugs such as pilocarpine. 
• Adverse effects: muscarinic overstimulation typically manifest 
within 15–30 minutes and include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, 
sweating, hypersalivation, cutaneous flushing, reflex tachycardia 
(sometimes bradycardia), and bronchoconstriction. 
• Intoxication with these agents can be treated by competitive 
blockade using Atropine.

Screening Laboratory Tests
•
Prothrombin time (PT) measurement
*The time that takes plasma to clot after addition of tissue factor.
1. Blood is drawn into a test tube containing liquid sodium citrate, which acts as an anticoagulant by 
binding Ca2+.
2. Centrifuge to separate out blood cells from plasma.
3. Add Ca2+ into the collected plasma to reverse sodium citrate action.
4. Add tissue factor (factor III) to activate the extrinsic clotting cascade pathway at 37°C.
5. Measure the clotting time (PT, usually 10 – 14 sec)
•
INR (International Normalized Ratio)
* Used primarily to monitor vitamin K antagonists (e.g., warfarin); INR does not measure 
DOAC activity.
* Healthy person not using warfarin: INR of 0.8–1.2
* People on warfarin therapy: INR of 2.0–3.0
1. Get the ratio of a patient's PT to a normal sample’s PT
2. The ratio raised to the power of the ISI value (assigned by tissue factor’s manufacturer)

1) General Features
· potent vasoactive agent
· effective topical hemostatic agent for control of bleeding from capillaries and arterioles
· can not control venous oozing or bleeding from larger vessels
· clinical use is limited to superficial bleeding
· levorotatory form is 15 times more potent than dextrorotatory isomer
2) Clinical Use
· Impregnated retraction cord
3) Preparation
· Epinephrine 1:1,000 (1 mg/mL; e.g., Bosmin); not racemic
4) Precautions
· serious cardiovascular reactions can be induced
· can retard wound healing
· can provoke local tissue damage from ischemia and even tissue necrosis
Topical Vasocontrictor: Epinephrine

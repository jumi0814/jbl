3. Cross-Linking of Fibrin by the Coagulation Cascade
Hemostasis Physiology
The clotting system consists of a cascade of proteolytic enzymes and cofactors.
• Inactive precursors are activated in series, each giving rise to more of the next : 
“Mechanism of Amplification”
• Thrombin (factor IIa) converts fibrinogen (factor I) into fibrin; activated factor XIII (XIIIa) 
cross-links fibrin to stabilize the clot.
• There are two pathways in the cascade:
- The extrinsic pathway, which operate in vivo
- The intrinsic or contact pathway, which operates in vitro
• Both pathways converge on factor X activation; factor Xa, with factor Va, converts 
prothrombin to thrombin.

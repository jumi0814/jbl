Antagonism (Chemical)
➢Hepatotoxicity from Acute overdose of acetaminophen (N-
acetyl-p-aminophenol)
✓Normal dose → NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine) reacts
rapidly with glutathione
✓Overdose → Glutathione is depleted, and NAPQI accumulates 
and results in alkylation of liver proteins and cellular injury. 
When enough liver cells are damaged, clinical signs of 
hepatotoxicity, such as nausea and jaundice, appear.
➢The antidote, N-acetylcysteine (NAC), acts as a precursor for 
glutathione and enables the formation of new glutathione and 
dramatically reduces mortality when administered within 8 - 10 
hrs after acute acetaminophen ingestion.
❖The FDA also recently banned prescription drugs from containing 
more than 325 mg of acetaminophen per dose. 
❖Some combinations of acetaminophen have been banned in 
the UK and studies have shown that this ban has reduced the 
number of suicide deaths in the UK.

Potential Adverse Effects of Drugs with Anticholinergic Properties in 
Geriatric and Cognitively Impaired Patients
• Drug-related anticholinergic adverse effects are potentially hazardous to elderly patients, especially those with 
cognitive impairment. 
• Additive anticholinergic effects from medications can compromise the safety of geriatric pts because: 
ü polypharmacy is a common practice in the geriatric population
ü many common drugs possess at least a small measure of anticholinergic activity 
ü the elderly, and especially cognitively impaired, are exquisitely sensitive to cholinergic blockade due to 
central cholinergic hypofunction and dysfunction in aging and dementia, respectively
• It should be noted that many over-the-counter medications have anticholinergic effects.
ü Diphenhydramine: an antihistamine with anticholinergic properties, which is often also used as a hypnotic, 
can cause confusion and cognitive dysfunction in the elderly and cognitively impaired pts. 
• Adverse effects from anticholinergic drugs in the elderly may include: 
ü acute encephalopathy (delirium, confusional state), falls, urinary retention, constipation, and exacerbation 
and decompensation of underlying cognitive, functional, and behavioral deficits (particularly in patients with 
dementia).

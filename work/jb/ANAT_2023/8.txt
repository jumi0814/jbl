Common ways to identify the main trunk of the 
facial nerve(FN)
1. The tragal pointer:
- Junction of cartilaginous portion of 
external auditory meatus with skull, which 
is 5-6mm from stylomastoid foramen.
- Identify the tragus of the ear. Dissect down 
the cartilage of the tragus to its inferior 
end called the “tragal pointer”.
- FN trunk is found 1cm inferior, anterior, 
and medial to the pointer.
- The tragal pointer has a blunt and variable 
tip that can change with retraction. It is 
probably the latest consistent landmark to 
use, as it does not point in the appropriate 
direction in some 80% of cases.
2. The posterior belly of the digastric muscle
- FN trunk can be found immediately 
superior to the upper border of the muscle 
and at the same depth. 
3. Tympanomastoid (TM) suture
- Junction of posterior bony auditory canal 
and mastoid portion of temporal bone
- May not be visualized and can only be 
detected on palpation.
- FN trunk is usually located approximately 
2-4mm deep to the inferior end of the TM 
suture line.
4. The styloid process
- FN trunk is located in the inferior and 
superficial soft tissue.
- The styloid process should be used with 
caution because it is situated immediately 
deep to the nerve.
